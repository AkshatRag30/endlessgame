1The game in 60 seconds

Five to ten friends crew a neon data station. One or two of you are secretly Glitches trying to break it from the inside. The crew fixes the station; the Glitches pick people off. Every so often everyone stops, argues, and votes someone out.

Join with a code→ Get your secret role→ Roam, do tasks, strike→ Body found or button pressed→ Talk and vote→ Back to roaming until someone wins

A match takes about 8 to 12 minutes. When it ends everyone sees who was who, and the host can start a rematch with one tap without anyone leaving the room.

You need: a phone or computer with a browser, 5 to 10 people, and ideally a voice call or the same room so you can actually argue. There is no install and no account; a nickname is enough.

2Getting into a room

Rooms are private. One person hosts and everyone else joins with a four letter code.

  1. Host: open the game, type a nickname, pick a colour, and tap Create room. You get a four letter code like KXPT and a share link.
  2. Everyone else: open the link, or open the game and type the code into Join. Codes never use the letters I, L or O, so you cannot mix them up with 1 or 0.
  3. In the lobby, pick your colour and an accessory, then tap Ready.
  4. The host checks the settings (see Host settings) and taps Start match once at least five players are in and everyone is ready.
Fewer than five of you? The host can turn on Practice mode in the settings, which allows a match with three or more, and tap + Add bot to fill seats with computer players. Bots walk around, do tasks, report bodies, vote, and if a bot is the Glitch, it hunts. Two friends plus three bots makes a decent match.
Dropped out? If your signal dies mid match, reopen the game within 60 seconds and you slide back into your slot with your role intact. Empty rooms close by themselves after 10 minutes.

3Your role

When the match starts a full screen card shows your role. Keep your screen to yourself.

Crew

Most players. You do tasks around the station, fix sabotage, report bodies, and vote out whoever you think is a Glitch. You get one emergency meeting per match.

You win when every crew task is done, or every Glitch has been voted out.

Glitch

One Glitch with 5 to 7 players, two with 8 to 10. You see who your partner is. You can eliminate crew, slip through conduits, sabotage systems and pretend to do tasks.

You win when Glitches equal or outnumber the crew, or a critical sabotage timer runs out.

Ghost

What you become after being eliminated or voted out. You keep playing quietly in the background and can only chat with other ghosts.

You win when your living team wins.

The Use button changes its label to whatever is in reach: a task name, Fix lights, Hold panel, Emergency meeting or, for Glitches, Conduit. Your own task consoles glow amber on the map.

Everyone walks at the same speed, about four and a half tiles a second, so a full lap of the station takes roughly 40 seconds. That number matters: if someone claims they crossed the whole map in ten seconds, they did not.

6Light and vision

You only see a circle of light around yourself, and walls block it. If someone is behind a wall, your game genuinely does not know where they are.

WhoHow far they see
Crew6 tiles
Glitch8 tiles, so they spot you before you spot them
Crew during a blackout2 tiles, until the lights are fixed
No wallhacks: the server never sends your phone the position of anyone you cannot see, so there is nothing hidden to dig out of the game.

7Tasks

Tasks are tiny touch minigames, 3 to 10 seconds each. Every crew member gets two common, three short and one long task. Finishing them fills the shared task bar at the top of the screen.

TaskWhereTypeHow to do it
Reroute CircuitPower GridShortDrag each of the four glowing wires to the port of the same colour.

Defrag BlocksServer VaultShortSlide the coloured blocks so each column holds one colour.

Common tasks are the same for every crew member, which makes them handy for checking stories: if someone says they did Sort Packets, everyone else has it too.

Prove yourself: some tasks light up something on the map when finished, like a panel turning green. If someone watches you complete one of those, you are almost certainly crew, because a Glitch can stand at a task but can never make the bar move.
Watch the bar during Jam Comms: when comms are jammed the task list and bar vanish for everyone until the dish is realigned, so nobody can use the bar as evidence.

8Playing as a Glitch

You win by patience. Strike when nobody is watching, use sabotage to split the crew up, and have a believable story ready for the meeting.

Your kit

ToolHow it works
Fake task listYou get a list that looks like a crew list. Stand at those spots and look busy; your task bar never moves.
EliminateWorks within 1.2 tiles, so you have to be right next to someone. After each one you wait 25 seconds (the host can set 15 to 45). The body stays where it fell until a meeting.
ConduitsSix grates linked in three pairs: Power Grid ↔ Cooling Bay, Dock ↔ Server Vault, Lounge ↔ Signal Tower. Only Glitches can use them (they glow violet for you). Going in and coming out flashes a purple ring that any crewmate watching either grate can see, so check the room is empty first.
SabotageFour kinds, explained in the next section. Only one can run at a time, with a shared 30 second cooldown.
Combo: trigger a blackout, then walk into the Power Grid crowd with your 8 tile vision while they see only 2 tiles. Or call Core Meltdown to pull two people into the Core Reactor and leave the far rooms empty.

Meltdown rule of thumb: the two closest people sprint to the Core Reactor and everyone else stays put. If you arrive and the other panel is held by someone, remember who; a Glitch might let go to make you fail.

10Meetings and voting

This is where matches are won. A meeting pulls everyone to a table, living players talk, then vote.

Starting a meeting

Either report a body you walk past, or press the emergency button in the Hub. Each player gets one emergency press per match, the button rests for 20 seconds after every meeting, and it cannot be used during a Core Meltdown.

How it runs

  1. Discussion, 45 seconds. Voting is locked. Share where you were and what you saw.
  2. Voting, 60 seconds. Tap a player card to vote for them, or tap Skip. Nobody sees votes until the end.
  3. Result. The player with the most votes is ejected. A tie, or skip winning, ejects nobody. Depending on the host setting, the screen may reveal whether the ejected player was a Glitch.

Quick chat

You can build messages by tapping instead of typing, which is much faster on a phone:

I was in [room]I saw [player] at [room][player] is susI did [task]Skip

In private rooms the host can also allow typed chat: 80 characters per message, with a swear filter. Every player card has mute and report.

11After you are eliminated

Being out does not mean being done. Ghosts float around and still help their team.

Crew ghosts keep doing their tasks, and those still fill the bar, so a dead crewmate can help win the match. Glitch ghosts can keep sabotaging. Ghosts chat only with other ghosts, so you cannot tip off the living.

House rule worth keeping: if you are on a voice call, ghosts stay muted. The game cannot stop you talking out loud, so this is on your honour.

12Strategy tips

For the crew

Move in pairs or threes, especially to the Dock and Cooling Bay. Remember who you saw and where, with rough times. Use the 40 second lap to check stories. Watch for anyone standing at a task without the panel lighting up. Do not burn your emergency press early; a body report is free.

For Glitches

Do your fake tasks where people pass by. Self report rarely, but sometimes. Vote with the crowd, not ahead of it. Never use a conduit with a light circle near you. With two Glitches, avoid being seen together right before a body is found.

At the table

Lead with facts (room, time, who you saw) before accusations. If two stories clash, ask a common task question. When unsure, skip; ejecting a crewmate brings the Glitches one step closer to outnumbering you.

14Fair play and safety

Glitch Station is for players aged 13 and up and is built so a single leaked secret cannot ruin a match.

Your phone only ever learns your own role (Glitches also learn their partner's). The server checks every move and every elimination, so speed tricks or eliminating from across the room simply get refused. The game only asks for a nickname: no email, no direct messages, no chatting with strangers. Use mute and report freely, and hosts can kick anyone from the lobby.
