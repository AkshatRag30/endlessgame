1Overview and vision

Lane Siege is a third person, endless survival shooter where the player runs forward across five lanes, dodges obstacles, grabs weapon upgrades and holds off monsters that grow stronger every 30 seconds. One run lasts 3 to 8 minutes, and the goal is to survive longer than last time.

AttributeDecision
GenreEndless lane runner crossed with an auto aim survival shooter
ViewThird person, camera behind and slightly above the player
PlatformWeb first (desktop and mobile browser, portrait on mobile), then wrapped for Android and iOS
Session length3 to 8 minutes per run, 15 to 25 minutes per sitting
AudienceCasual and midcore players aged 14 to 35 who like Subway Surfers, Vampire Survivors and Archero
MonetizationNFT hero sales and secondary royalties, plus optional rewarded ads for a revive; no pay to win

The design pillar is “one more run”: every death should feel fair, every run should show visible power growth, and the next unlock should always be in sight.

3Core gameplay loop

The player only steers; the gun fires automatically at the nearest threat, so all skill goes into lane choice, timing and which upgrade gate to run through.

Moment to moment (every 2 to 5 seconds)

  1. Read the next row of obstacles and monsters coming down the lanes.
  2. Switch lanes to dodge, or stay in a lane to focus fire on a dangerous monster.
  3. Pick up coins, XP orbs and weapon crates dropped by kills.
  4. Choose between two upgrade gates side by side, for example “+2 bullets” or “+30% fire rate”.

Run loop (every 30 seconds)

  1. A new wave starts and monster types or stats step up.
  2. Every 3rd wave a mini boss blocks the centre lanes.
  3. Every 5th wave a full boss fight drops a guaranteed rare weapon.
  4. The run ends when health reaches zero; score is time survived plus kills.

Meta loop (between runs)

  1. Spend coins on permanent upgrades such as base health, starting weapon and magnet range.
  2. Unlock new heroes and weapon types by hitting milestones.
  3. Complete 3 daily missions for bonus coins.

4Controls and camera

Controls must work with one thumb on mobile and two keys on desktop.

ActionMobileDesktop
Move one lane left or rightSwipe left or rightA / D or arrow keys
Jump over low obstaclesSwipe upW, Space or up arrow
Slide under high obstaclesSwipe downS or down arrow
Use special ability (charged)Double tapE
PauseTap pause iconEsc

Shooting is automatic and targets the closest monster in the current lane first, then adjacent lanes. Lane switches take 0.15 seconds with an eased tween so movement feels snappy but readable.

The camera sits 6 units behind and 3.5 units above the hero, tilted 18 degrees down, so players see roughly 40 units ahead. In portrait it sits higher and frames the lanes narrower. It shakes briefly on big hits and boss spawns, and pulls back 10% during boss fights.

The track has 5 lanes; obstacles spawn in rows so at least one lane is always passable, which keeps every death the player’s fault rather than the game’s.

A pattern generator picks from hand built row templates weighted by wave number. The gap between rows starts at 25 units and shrinks to 14 units by wave 15. No two hard rows spawn back to back, and every 8th row is a breather row with only pickups.

6Weapons, levels and upgrades

Weapons unlock by level inside each run: every kill gives XP, and each level up puts a weapon gate pair on the track, one side offering a new or different gun and the other upgrading the current gun’s tier.

XP per kill: Crawler and Runner 1, Brute 4, Elite +3 extra, boss 20. XP needed for the next level is 5 + 4 × current level, so the Rocket Launcher usually arrives around wave 3 or 4 and the Black Hole Cannon needs a strong run. Weapon gates wait for a clear stretch of track so they never overlap upgrade gates.

8Difficulty curve

Difficulty rises every 30 second wave on a gentle exponential, tuned so an average new player dies around wave 6 (3 minutes) and a skilled player reaches wave 15 or beyond.

HP(n) = HPbase × 1.12n−1
Spawns(n) = 6 + 2n + ⌊n1.3 ÷ 2⌋

Run speed rises continuously, not per wave: it starts at 14 units per second and climbs by 0.07 every second, reaching 18 at one minute, 22 at two, 27 at three and the cap of 34 around 4:45. Because rows and monsters spawn at fixed distances, faster running also means less reaction time. The camera field of view widens from 62 to about 71 degrees as speed rises so the rush is felt, and each new wave shows “speed up” with the wave number.

WaveTimeMonster HP multiplierNew threat
10:001.0xCrawlers, barricades
52:001.6xIron Golem boss, Spitters
104:302.8xHive Queen, collapsing floors
157:004.9xVoid Wyrm, mixed elite packs
209:308.6xRemix bosses, endless scaling

A dynamic difficulty assist quietly lowers spawn count by up to 15% for players who die before wave 3 three runs in a row, so new players are not lost early.

10HUD, game feel and audio

The screen stays clean so the lanes are always readable: HUD panels are translucent glass at the edges, and big moments get banners rather than clutter.

Timer and waveTop centreTimer sits inside a ring that fills as the current 30 second wave progresses

Combo counterRight sideAppears from 3 chained kills
Boss health barBelow timerOnly during boss fights

Game feel: 40 ms hit stop on heavy hits, floating damage numbers (crits in large gold), white hit flashes, glowing projectiles with muzzle flashes and impact flares, a light trail behind the hero in its accent colour, health bars over Brutes and Elites, and glowing auras on Elites. Wave, level and boss moments get large animated banners.

World style: synthwave sky gradient with stars and a striped sunset sun, a scrolling neon grid beside the track, mountains outlined in neon, glowing rails and light posts. Speed streaks and a tinted screen edge appear as run speed rises.

Menus: the main menu shows the chosen hero in 3D facing the camera, a grid of four hero cards, and an armory of nine weapon tiles that show their unlock level and describe the weapon when tapped. Starting a run swoops the camera behind the hero. The death screen counts up kills, coins, best combo and level reached, and animates progress toward the next hero.

Audio: a driving synthwave track that layers in extra instruments every 5 waves, distinct sounds per weapon, and a clear cue 1 second before any lane wide attack so players can react by sound alone. The prototype uses simple synthesized sounds as placeholders.

11Web3 and NFT heroes

Every hero is an NFT the player owns, while the game stays fully playable without a wallet so Web3 never blocks the first run.

AreaDecision
Hero NFTsEach hero is an ERC721 token; metadata holds name, skin, rarity, trail effect and lifetime stats like best wave
FairnessCosmetic and identity only in v1; same base stats for every hero, so no pay to win
Free entryA free starter hero lives off chain; players can claim it as an NFT once they connect a wallet
WalletOptional connect via WalletConnect and injected wallets (MetaMask, Coinbase Wallet); guest play with local saves until then
ChainA low fee EVM chain (Base or Polygon shortlisted) so minting and trading cost cents
MintingLazy minting on claim, with gas sponsored for the starter hero
Earning heroesMilestone heroes (for example “reach wave 15”) become claimable, verified by a server signed proof of the run
TradingTradable on standard marketplaces; a royalty on secondary sales funds development
Anti cheatRun results validated server side before any on chain claim, so modded clients cannot farm NFTs

Endless Domains integration (future): once the SDK is ready, a player’s Endless domain can act as their in game name and profile, shown on the death screen and later on leaderboards, with their hero NFTs displayed on that profile. This phase starts with a review of what the SDK exposes. Until then, Basenames and ENS provide the same experience through a provider layer (Phase 8) that Endless plugs into as the first provider.

Web3 rollout

  1. v1: guest play, wallet connect, starter hero claim, 3 to 5 mintable heroes.
  2. v1.5: milestone hero claims with signed run proofs, marketplace listing, plus Basename and ENS identity, Sign In With Ethereum, profile pages, holder perks and soulbound badges.
  3. v2: Endless Domains identity, leaderboards keyed to domain names, domain linked profiles.

Open Web3 questions

  1. Base or Polygon, or the chain Endless Domains runs on, to keep one ecosystem?
  2. Fixed supply per hero for scarcity, or open editions with rarity tiers?
  3. Should the starter hero claim be free with sponsored gas, or tied to a domain claim?

Phase 8: Onchain identity before Endless 1 to 1.5 weeks Medium

Give players the “domain as identity” experience now with Basenames and ENS, through a provider layer that Endless Domains slots into later. Needs Phases 6 and 7.

Cost: $0Reading Basenames and ENS is free through the same free RPC tier; badges stay on testnet until launch.
Step by step
  1. Add a private RPC provider (for example Alchemy or QuickNode) for Ethereum mainnet and Base, stored in environment variables; public RPCs are too slow for name resolution.
  2. Create src/identity/types.ts with a PlayerIdentity type: address, displayName, avatarUrl and source (basename, ens or address).
  3. Write one provider file per name system in src/identity/providers: basenameProvider uses viem getEnsName with the Base coin type, ensProvider uses getEnsName and getEnsAvatar on mainnet, addressProvider returns the short address. Each exposes resolve(address) and returns an identity or null.
  4. Write resolveIdentity(address) in src/identity/index.ts: it tries providers in the order listed in src/data/identity.json, gives each a 2 second timeout, and caches the result for 24 hours.
  5. Add Sign In With Ethereum: a siwe_nonce Edge Function issues a one time nonce, the player signs a message, and a siwe_verify Edge Function checks the signature and links the wallet to their Supabase player, merging any guest progress.
  6. Resolve identity on the server too, inside siwe_verify and a daily refresh job, and store display_name, avatar_url and identity_source on the players table so leaderboards never resolve names in the browser.
  7. Show the identity everywhere through one IdentityChip component: hero select header, leaderboard rows, death screen and the share card.
  8. Add the NFT avatar picker: an Edge Function lists NFTs the wallet holds via an indexer API, the player picks one, and the daily job drops it if the wallet no longer owns it.
  9. Build public profile pages at /p/:name: a page showing name, avatar, owned heroes, best wave and badges, plus a Cloudflare Pages Function that renders a social preview image.
  10. Add holder perks from src/data/perks.json (chain, contract address, cosmetic reward); siwe_verify checks balances and unlocks only cosmetics.
  11. Write the soulbound badge contract: an ERC721 that blocks transfers and reports locked status (ERC5192), minted with the same server signed vouchers as heroes, deployed to Base Sepolia with Foundry tests.
  12. Add tests: provider order, fallbacks, timeouts, cache expiry, SIWE replay protection, and a fake provider proving a new name system plugs in with one file and one line in identity.json.
Done when

  • A holder perk unlocks for a test wallet, and a badge mints on Base Sepolia and cannot be transferred.
